# Wickwarden — story sketch

Working title: **Wickwarden**. The world event is called **the Lanternfall**.

This story is original. It shares no characters, places, objects or plot structure with any
Nintendo game. The game shares only mechanics with classic top-down adventures: free
exploration, sub-items, hidden passages and dungeons with keys and a boss.

## Premise

The valley of **Vey** lived for a thousand years under the **Nine Lanterns**. These are
enormous lights that drift slowly above the land. Each lantern keeps one thing alive in the
valley: warmth, growth, rain, sleep, memory, song, courage, trade and the turn of the seasons.

One night all nine lanterns fell. They did not break. They burned their way deep into the
ground, and each one became an **Underlight**: a crater, a ruin or a cave that grew around the
fallen light. Since that night, the valley is in a slow dusk that does not end.

In the dusk, the **Hush** came. The Hush are creatures made of things the valley forgot: old
songs, lost tools, unburied grudges, wax from candles that nobody lit. They are not evil. They
are hungry, and the light of a lantern is the only thing they want.

## The protagonist

**Tamsin Ode** is an apprentice lamplighter in the village of **Brackwater Ford**. Her work was
small: she lit the street lamps at night and trimmed the wicks in the morning. Her master,
**Old Corrow**, was the last **Wickwarden**: the person whose duty was to climb the lantern
towers and tend the Nine Lanterns.

Corrow disappeared on the night of the Lanternfall. He left behind his **lamp hook**, a long
brass pole with a hooked end, and a lamp that will not go out. Tamsin takes them both.

## The quest

Tamsin goes down into each Underlight, finds the core of the fallen lantern and rekindles it
with her lamp. Each rekindled lantern gives a part of its old power back to the valley, and it
gives Tamsin a tool that opens new paths.

The valley people believe that the Hush pulled the lanterns down. Tamsin finds a different
truth step by step, in murals, in letters from Corrow and in the memories that each lantern
shows her when she rekindles it.

## The truth (revealed late)

The lanterns did not fall by accident. Corrow pulled them down himself. The lanterns were not
gifts. They fed on the valley: each one took a small part of every life it kept warm. The
Hush are the remains of what the lanterns took over a thousand years.

Corrow did not have the strength to put out the lanterns. He only had the strength to bury
them. He went into the deepest Underlight, **the Ninth Hollow**, to keep the last lantern
asleep.

## The ending

In the Ninth Hollow, Tamsin finds Corrow, half wax and half man. She must choose:

1. **Rekindle the last lantern.** The valley gets its light back, and the old cost comes back
   with it.
2. **Become the Wickwarden.** Tamsin puts out the lantern and lets the valley find its own light.
   The dusk changes slowly into a real dawn. The Hush settle and become quiet.

There is no evil king and no captured person to save. The conflict is about the cost of comfort.

## Tone

Warm, melancholic and curious. Painterly, high-detail art with strong light against deep dusk.
Light is the main visual theme: every lamp, torch and fire in the world means something.
